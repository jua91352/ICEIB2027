\begin{abstract}
% A = Attention getter (motivation)
After major typhoons and heavy rain in Taiwan, average wholesale vegetable
prices have risen by about 10--40\% in the first week and by up to about
100\% in the third week, yet a shopper who sees today's shelf price has no
tool that says whether it will rise or fall.
% B = But (challenge)
Published market averages are not the price of an individual store, retail
prices stay unchanged most of the time and then move abruptly, and large
language models (LLMs) asked about prices directly may produce unsupported
numbers.
% C = Cure (proposed solution)
We propose the Fresh Market Price Forecast Agent (FMPA), a domain AI agent
that anchors short-term forecasts on the store price the user observes.
% D = Development (method)
FMPA restricts the LLM to intent parsing and explanation, obtains every number
through Model Context Protocol (MCP) tools, and estimates the relative price
change at 7 and 14 calendar days with RandomForest and HistGradientBoosting
models, applied either directly to the current price (mode A) or with a fixed
weekly-momentum adjustment (mode B).
% E = Experiments (evaluation)
We evaluate FMPA on historical prices from 13 public markets in Taichung City
with a leakage-safe chronological split (2,085 seven-day and 1,859
fourteen-day test samples), against no-change and momentum baselines, using
direction macro-F1 and the mean absolute error of the relative change.
% F = Findings
The learned models did not outperform these simple baselines, which is
consistent with sticky retail prices, so we do not claim superiority over
them. Future work will add feature engineering, event indicators, and
uncertainty estimates, and will validate forecasts against matured same-store
prices.
\end{abstract}
